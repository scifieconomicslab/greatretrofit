## 1. EPC / PEB data (Brussels) — **confidence: high for what it measures; low as a stock estimate**

Source: Bruxelles Environnement, "Rapport statistique – données 2025" of PEB residential certificates (27 pp.), downloaded from https://environnement.brussels/media/22310/download?inline [R]; landing page https://environnement.brussels/citoyen/reglementation-et-inspection/obligations-et-autorisations/les-statistiques-des-certificats-peb-des-logements-bruxellois (contact info-certibru@environnement.brussels; the 2024 data edition is at https://document.environnement.brussels/opac_css/elecfile/Statistiques_certificatsPEB_residentiel_donnees_2024.pdf per that page). Format: PDF report with tables; **no open row-level data found**. Licence: not stated.

Facts read in the report [R]:
- 447,369 certificates established from 2011 to 01/01/2026 (existing and new dwellings); 417,532 for existing dwellings; 390,858 certificates are apartments and 56,511 houses. 291,472 were valid on 01/01/2026; 46.11% of dwellings had a valid certificate. **Jette: 40.9% of dwellings had a valid certificate**, 59.1% not.
- The report warns the sample is not statistically representative of the Brussels stock (certificates arise mostly at sale/rental, often before works).
- Class distribution of all 447,369 certificates (primary energy, kWh/m²/yr): A 1.71%, B 5.84%, C 12.41%, D 19.46%, E 18.85%, F 13.84%, G 27.89%. 58.3% meet the 275 kWh/m²/yr target (class E or better); 20% meet 150.
- Class bounds for existing individual dwellings (from the protocol, via search summary [S]): A ≤45, B 46–95, C 96–150, D 151–210, E 211–275, F 276–345, G >345.
- **Jette, valid certificates in 2025, class shares [R]:** A 0.66%, B 4.86%, C 14.08%, D 20.20%, E 18.24%, F 13.17%, G 28.77%.
- Average specific consumption (primary energy, kWh/m²/yr) of existing dwellings by build period (all types combined) [R]: before 1900: 340.61; 1900–09: 351.65; 1910–19: 345.56; 1920–29: 345.72; 1930–39: 348.20; 1940–49: 360.91; 1950–59: 333.12; 1960–69: 308.48; 1970–79: 246.53; 1980–89: 216.23; 1990–99: 194.62; 2000–09: 164.88; 2010–19: 129.2; 2020–25: 100.9. Apartments are lower than houses (e.g. 1930–39: apartment 329.19, 2-façade house 365.59). The report states values for older periods partly reflect calculation defaults tied to build period.
- Mean EPC result fell 23% between 2011 and 2025 (certification year); standard deviation across certificates 212.37 kWh/m²/yr [R].
- Jette's housing is mostly pre-1946 (≈60% of residential buildings per IBSA; see `tenure-and-buildings.md`), which maps to the 330–360 kWh/m²/yr band above — an inference, not a Jette-specific measurement.
- **Modelled savings from recommended works** (EPC calculation, not measured), apartments [R], mean gain in kWh/m²/yr when the work is in the top-3 recommendations: insulate façade 77.79; insulate flat roof 143.83; insulate sloping roof 294.46; insulate floor 68.82; replace windows 31.98; add ventilation 32.41; install central heating 162.56. Average gain from the first three recommended works across certificates 2017–2025: 61.10 kWh/m²/yr (std dev 86.06). A house table exists in the same report (not extracted).
- Insulation presence in walls/roofs/floors by type and the share with unknown insulation are in the report (sections on walls p. ~16 onward); not extracted.
- Brussels targets: PEB 275 kWh/m²/yr for all dwellings by 2033, PEB 150 longer term [R]; 100 kWh/m²/yr average by 2050 (INNOVATE case study) [R, see §4].
- Region-wide average for houses 363 kWh/m² in Q1 2024 and 2050 target 100, from a Belgian renovation-rate study on ORBi (https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/343226/1/Belgium_Renovation_Rates_v3.docx) [S]; not opened — low confidence.

## 2. Cost per m² and per dwelling — mostly **not found**

- **No source found** giving observed retrofit cost per m² for Brussels dwellings, nor a paired cost-and-savings dataset. Checked: Bruxelles Environnement EPC reports (no costs), Renolution documents (premium schedule only, not project costs), BISA Focus 83 press release (premium totals only).
- Per-unit figure for condominiums: the EU INNOVATE case study "Brussels Region" (2020) states "Deep renovation in medium-sized condominiums: €30,000 – 40,000 per unit. For smaller buildings, the cost per household tends to be higher, for larger buildings, lower." [R] https://energy-cities.eu/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Brussels-Region_INNOVATE-case-study_2020-en.pdf — targets condominiums with 10–50 units. This is a programme-design estimate, not a measured average. Confidence: medium-low.
- Cost data points from other places (not Brussels) found in search summaries [S]: a cost-benefit comparison of two regional programmes with average retrofit costs of 290 and 415 €/m² and savings of 63 and 88 kWh/m² final energy (https://hal-edf.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01826170 — which regions was not verified); Energiesprong whole-house costs of about £75,000 (Nottingham), EUR 75,000 in France with a EUR 45,000 target (Ashden / Green Alliance pages in search results). Advocacy-grade; use only as ranges.
- Indirect handle (derivable): the Renolution schedule below fixes subsidy per m² for insulation (e.g. façade insulation from outside €50/70/90 per m² by income category). If the premium covers a known share of cost, an implied cost could be derived — the share is not in the schedule. Not done.
- Recommended next step: ask Bruxelles Environnement / Homegrade for anonymised Renolution application data (invoice amount vs premium, per work type), which would give actual cost per m² and income category — the BISA Focus 83 authors (perspective.brussels) already worked from such data.

## 3. Brussels support schemes

### Renolution premiums — **status: closed/transitional; confidence: medium-high**
- Merged the former energy, housing-renovation and façade premiums on 1 Jan 2022 [R] (https://be.brussels/fr/logement/construction-et-renovation/primes-fiscalite-et-financement-la-renovation).
- Status [R]: the same page says "A ce jour, il n'y a pas de décision gouvernementale concernant d'éventuelles Primes RENOLUTION 2025 et/ou 2026" and no application can be lodged for final invoices dated 2025 or 2026. Brussels Times (2026) [R] (https://www.brusselstimes.com/brussels/2060611/the-wait-is-over-brussels-starts-paying-out-long-overdue-renovation-grants/): the scheme ended 31 Dec 2024; payments were frozen for lack of a functioning government; a budget of about €56 million approved by Parliament on 27 March 2026 released; 2,967 approved applications worth €45 million ready for payment; payments expected April–May 2026; "the new government is preparing an interest-free loan for future renovations" with measures not finalised.
- Another source says applications were suspended from 16 August 2025 due to demand (bx1, search summary) [S] — conflicts with the above on dates; treat the timeline as uncertain.
- Take-up (BISA Focus 83, press release 13 July 2026) [R] https://bisa.brussels/sites/default/files/2026-07/PB_Focus83_lay.pdf: almost €279 million invested through the premiums 2022–2025; almost 80% of the amount went to energy-performance works (mainly roof and façade insulation and roof waterproofing); owner-occupiers received 51% of amounts awarded; single-family houses 44% (vs 14% of dwellings); uptake higher in affluent south-east neighbourhoods even after controlling for dwelling type and tenure. Full Focus (Dutch) "Ongelijkheden bij de steun voor woningrenovatie in Brussel?" not downloaded.
- **2024 schedule (the last official one I read)** — "Tableau récapitulatif des primes RENOLUTION 2024" (PDF created 6 March 2024) [R] https://renolution.brussels/sites/default/files/media-documents/tableau_recapitulatif_primes_renolution_2024.pdf. Amounts by income category I / II / III. The table does not define the categories; amounts rise from I to III, and a 2022 news item says the roof-insulation premium rose in category III ("low" incomes) from 55 to 75 €/m² [S, search summary of dhnet.be], so III is most likely the lowest-income category. The housing-service.be guide below describes category I as the *modest*-income, highest-premium category, which contradicts this — **unresolved; check renolution.brussels**. Selected lines:
  - Roof thermal insulation (E3): €35 / 55 / 75 per m².
  - Façade insulation (F1): from the inside €35/40/45; from the outside €50/70/90; cavity €20/25/30, per m².
  - Floor insulation (H1): €35/40/45 per m². Windows (G1) wood €100/120/140, PVC/metal €40/50/55 per m².
  - Air/water heat pump (J4): €4,500 / 4,750 / 5,000 per dwelling; ground/water source €5,800 / 6,150 / 6,500. Solar water heater (J8) €2,500/3,000/3,500.
  - Ventilation system C (M1) €1,550/1,950/2,230 per dwelling; system D (M2) €3,000/3,750/4,300.
  - Energy audit (A1): €400 per single-family house, €3,000 per apartment building. Scaffolding (B1): €20/30/40 per m². Structure, humidity, roof structure lines are % of eligible cost (e.g. 30/50/70%).
  - Bonuses exist (durable insulation material, several works, exit from oil/coal).
- Rules from a secondary 2026 guide (housing-service.be) [S] https://www.housing-service.be/prime-renovation-bruxelles-2026/, flagged by that page itself as provisional: income thresholds ≈ €35,000 (single, +per dependant) and ≈ €75,000 (that guide's I/II/III labelling conflicts with the official table's pattern); global cap €90,000 per dwelling (the page is inconsistent whether also per beneficiary); an extra 10% in the EDRLR zone; apply within 12 months of the final invoice, no pre-approval needed; acknowledgement in 15 days, administrative check in 60 days, payment within 90 days of a favourable decision, i.e. 4–6 months overall. The official income-category thresholds were **not found in an official document**. Do not present these cap/threshold numbers as official.
- Payout timing in practice: the 2024 backlog was paid in spring 2026, i.e. well over a year after final invoices (Brussels Times above). The January 2024 sources said applications are filed after works (post-payment reimbursement), so households fronted the full cost.

### Loans — **confidence: low-medium**
- ECORENO credit: preferential rate 2.5%–3.5% for owners, future owners, tenants; covers insulation, ventilation, heating, PV, heat pumps, solar water heaters [R] (be.brussels page above). Caps not listed there.
- Prêt Vert Bruxellois: a Bruxelles Environnement leaflet describes a rate of 0–2% for energy-efficiency works [S]; a Fonds du Logement document lists a maximum of €25,000 [S] (undated). A bx1 article reports the Fonds du Logement restricting access to households within social-housing income limits, with Ecoreno at 1.5% or 2.5% [S], date unconfirmed. Product page: https://homegrade.brussels/particuliers/pret-vert-bruxellois/ (not opened). Current terms: **verify**.
- Government plans an interest-free loan (Brussels Times above) — not yet defined.

## 4. Public / cooperative retrofit service precedents
- **Homegrade (Brussels Region, one-stop shop)** [R, INNOVATE case study; S, press kit]. Free advice and support for private owners and small co-ownerships (up to six units), with a dedicated one-stop shop for medium/large condominiums launched 2021 (pilot 2018 with four condominiums; coach, scenarios with costs, contract with the co-owners' association, free of charge in first years, Brussels Environment bearing 100% of costs; budget €1.8 million/yr for the Sustainable Building Facilitator and about €500,000/yr for the one-stop-shop team of 16, in the case study). No evaluation reporting cost per completed retrofit was found. Homegrade press kit 2024: https://homegrade.brussels/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/DOSSIER-DE-PRESSE-HOMEGRADE-FR-2024.pdf [S].
- Useful structural fact for the "public retrofit service" delivery mode: the INNOVATE case study describes the service as coaching + procurement coordination, not public execution; the owner association contracts the works.
- **Energiesprong** (NL/UK/FR): industrialised whole-house retrofit with guaranteed energy performance; costs reported in Dutch press as about $94,000 per row house, UK Nottingham pilots £75,000/home (vs £110,000 in NL in 2010 per the same source), France starting around €75,000 with a €45,000 target [S] (Ashden page https://www.ashden.org/winners/the-national-energy-foundation-and-energiesprong-uk; Green Alliance PDF https://green-alliance.org.uk/resources/reinventing_retrofit.pdf). Mostly social housing, not private owner-occupied. Treat as advocacy-sourced.
- Dutch "Nationaal Warmtefonds" and German analogues: not found in this survey.
